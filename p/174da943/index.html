All three make money. The difference is not the profit, it is how much CBH has to put in and how long it stays there. Selling returns ₹2.60 for every rupee in two years. Renting for ten returns ₹1.39 for every rupee in ten — less than a fixed deposit, on four times the money.

These figures assume the money still to be found comes in as equity. If it is borrowed at 18% instead, strategy 1 still works — profit falls from ₹98.7 to ₹88.8 crore. Strategies 2 and 3 do not: their gaps are too large and open for too long, and at 18% the interest alone would be ₹771 crore and ₹143 crore. Those two only exist with patient money behind them.

2
Renting for ten years earns almost the same profit on four times the money. ₹98.4 crore against ₹98.7 crore — but ₹250 crore has to sit in it for a decade instead of ₹62 crore for two years. The building is not the problem. The decade is.
3
The hybrid is the middle road and it costs ₹32 crore. It makes less than selling, takes two and a half times as long, and needs nearly twice the money up front. It is defensible if CBH wants rental income and control of the tenant mix. It is not the cheaper option.
i
How the hybrid actually works. Showrooms are let to brands on long leases, held for about eighteen months so the rent is proven, then sold one at a time to investors with the tenant and lease carried over. Nobody buys thirty-two showrooms at once. The model sells two showrooms and three offices a month from month 54, which is how these buildings are normally cleared on this corridor.

!
8% a year is not much of an offer. A fixed deposit pays around 7% with no vacancy risk, no tenant risk, and you can break it tomorrow. What makes investors accept a lower yield — and a lower yield is where CBH's money is on this route — is a registered long lease, a tenant name they recognise, escalation written in with dates, and a clean exit. None of those are in the model, and all of them are what the conversation with investors is actually about.

The question nobody has asked yet

The ₹36.8 crore still to be found is the largest unsettled item in this plan — larger than FSI, larger than the land schedule. Borrowed at 18% it costs ₹9.95 crore and CBH keeps ₹88.8 crore. Brought in as a partner it costs nothing in interest but buys a share of ₹98.7 crore. On a project returning ₹2.60 for every rupee in just over two years, anyone with ₹37 crore will want the share, not the interest. Decide what CBH is willing to give up before going out to look for the money, not during the conversation.

Open question · FSI

The carpet area on the plans is 2,05,124 sq ft. Turning that into an FSI figure depends on how efficient the layout is. At 80% carpet-to-FSI, which is what Rajhans Blue Cloud achieves, the plans imply 4.32 FSI. At 89%, which The Visit achieves, they imply 3.88. The permissible figure on a 45 m or 60 m road is 4.0. This is not a modelling question — it is a one-line question for the architect: what is the sanctioned FSI area? If the answer comes back at 4.0, saleable area falls by roughly the percentage above and sale value falls with it. The land cost does not.

Recommendation

Build it and sell it. Lease only the ground floor, and only if a brand signs before launch.

Our call

Sell the building off-plan (Strategy 1). It makes ₹98.7 crore and returns ₹2.60 for every rupee put in, in just over two years. Renting for ten years makes almost the same profit but returns ₹1.39 per rupee and ties up four times the money. The hybrid sits in between at ₹1.59 over five years. Before signing anything else, settle three things: the architect's sanctioned FSI area, whether the month 42 land instalment survives RERA, and what the ₹36.8 crore funder is being offered. None of those is a modelling question and all three change the answer.

Six moves, in the order they matter

01Reshape the land instalmentsMonths 6, 12 and 18 land while collections are thin. Making the early instalments smaller and the later ones bigger shrinks the funding gap without touching a single sales assumption. This is a conversation with the seller, and it is worth more than anything negotiable on construction cost.the cheapest lever on the table
02Get RERA registration to month 3The launch month is not a view about demand — it is the month money can legally be taken. Brokers are already working the corridor; none of it becomes cash until registration. Three months earlier is worth about ₹12 crore of funding gap.≈ ₹12 Cr
03Convert the broker interest into signed bookings25% pre-booked at launch instead of 10% is worth about ₹11 crore. The interest already exists. What is missing is the paperwork to convert it on day one.≈ ₹11 Cr
04Settle the gap funding before looking for it₹36.8 crore is needed, and the project returns ₹2.60 for every rupee in two years. Decide now whether that money is debt at a fixed rate or a partner with a share, and what the share is. Going out to look without an answer is how developers give away more than they need to.₹36.8 Cr
05Lease the ground floor, sell everything above itShowrooms are a coin flip at a 5.82% break-even yield against the 6.00% assumed, so leasing them is justified by the anchor and the address, not the arithmetic. Offices are not close: leasing those costs ₹35 crore. If no brand signs before launch, sell the ground floor too.decide before launch
06Tender the structure before the launch price is fixed₹2,500 a square foot came from CBH and has not been tendered. Cost would have to rise 86% to wipe out the profit, so this is not about protecting the margin — it is about knowing the real number before committing to a price list.before plans freeze

Four decision gates

Gate 1Before the land deed is signedSanctioned FSI area in writing. Month 42 instalment cleared with the seller and the RERA consultant. Stamp duty confirmed as inside or outside the ₹120 crore.
Gate 2Before launchGap funding committed on agreed terms. Structure tendered. At least one anchor term sheet signed, or the ground floor goes on the sale list.
Gate 3Months 1 and 2 after launchUnder eight units booked across both months and the 6.1 a month assumption is wrong. Re-price the upper floors or sell a floor in bulk rather than wait.
Gate 4Month 30Market money is due back at month 36. If collections are tracking below the model by more than 15%, line up the replacement now, not at month 35.
